sitting in riyadh at 3am watching the championship take shape, it hit me around halfway through the season: mercedes isn't just winning. they're suffocating the sport in a way that feels structurally broken.

andrea kimi antonelli is leading the championship with 171 points. george russell has 131. lewis hamilton has 125. that's 427 points between three drivers from one team. ferrari has 204 total. mclaren has 159. mercedes, as a constructor, has 302. they're lapping the field in a championship that's supposed to be competitive (or at least pretend to be).

here's the thing nobody wants to say out loud: this isn't competitive. this is a mercedes exhibition with guest appearances from other teams.

antonelli's season has been genuinely impressive (the kid can clearly drive), but his dominance is inseparable from machinery that's simply better. when your third-best driver is lewis hamilton and he's still fifth in points, something has gone wrong with competitive balance. the regulation book promised parity. instead, mercedes built something so fundamentally superior that even their mid-pack performances are podium-clinching.

the fia will say the cars are within the rules. technically, sure. but the spirit of formula 1 (if it has one anymore) is supposed to be about racing, not about which team got the 2026 power unit concept right by a bigger margin. max verstappen is seventh with 73 points. max verstappen. the guy who dominated multiple seasons and still has one of the most ruthless racecraft toolkits on the grid. he's seventh because his team built something that can't match mercedes' aero package, hybrid deployment, or basic straightline speed.

red bull falling this far isn't about incompetence. it's about mercedes' competence being so overwhelming that it's created a gravity well. everyone else is orbiting around it.

the constructors' championship tells the real story. mercedes has 302. ferrari, with leclerc and another solid driver, has 204. that's a 98-point gap at the halfway mark (roughly). in a sport that loves to talk about "exciting battles," this is the opposite of exciting. it's arithmetic. ferrari has to be perfect while mercedes can have an off weekend and still extend their lead.

what makes this genuinely frustrating from a viewer perspective is that the midfield is doing exactly what it should be doing. mclaren's piastri and norris are fighting, leclerc is aggressive and occasionally brilliant, gasly is grinding for points. but none of it matters because the top three are in a completely different championship. they're not racing each other most of the time (they're separated by point gaps that suggest different competitions). they're just collecting wins and podiums like it's their designated job.

silverstone this weekend might be the last chance to see something break that pattern (home circuits occasionally produce chaos, though mercedes will probably dominate there too). more likely, we'll watch antonelli extend his lead, russell secure another podium, and the paddock will call it "thrilling" because someone qualified third by half a tenth.

the 2026 regulations were supposed to fix this. instead, mercedes read the rules more carefully than everyone else, and now we're watching a championship that feels predetermined. that's not formula 1. that's a marketing exercise wearing formula 1's suit.
