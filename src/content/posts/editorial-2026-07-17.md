andrea kimi antonelli leads the championship by 25 points. mercedes leads the constructors by 78. on paper, this is dominance. in reality, watching from riyadh at 3am this season has felt less like watching a team in control and more like watching three drivers in a car park trying to figure out who has the keys.

the standings are lying to you (or maybe they're telling the truth and we're all just in denial). yes, antonelli is clear at the top. yes, russell and hamilton are second and third. but mercedes' margin in the constructors is inflated by the fact that they've locked out two of the top three spots. the moment you dig into race by race performance, the picture gets messy.

ferrari is 78 points back in the constructors, which sounds like a gulf. it isn't. leclerc is only 71 points behind antonelli in the drivers' championship. that's the margin between "clear leader" and "genuinely vulnerable." one bad weekend, one strategic miscalculation, and we're talking about a championship fight instead of a coronation.

here's what actually concerns me about mercedes: they're winning without having solved anything. antonelli is fast (genuinely, unfairly fast in a way that reminds you why he was always meant to be here), but the team around him feels like it's reacting rather than controlling. george russell is consistent, which is fine for a second-place car but not fine when you're supposed to be a championship-winning outfit. and lewis hamilton is still somewhere in that nebulous zone where he's quick enough to be dangerous but not quick enough to be the guy you want when it matters.

it's the constructors result that keeps me up. 333 points. mclaren is at 179 with two competitive drivers. ferrari is at 255 with leclerc doing genuinely interesting things and piastri's teammate (oscar) pulling his weight. mercedes should be lapping these teams if antonelli is this dominant. instead, the gap to ferrari is tighter than the gap suggests it should be.

spa this weekend will tell us something real. it's a power circuit where you can't hide. mercedes' dominance here will either confirm that this is a genuine engineering advantage or reveal that antonelli is simply out-driving a car that's fine, not exceptional.

the dangerous thing about mercedes' position isn't that they might lose the drivers' championship (antonelli would have to catastrophically implode). it's that they might win both championships while ferrari picks up wins and momentum and williams slowly assembles something threatening for next year. you can win a season and still feel like you lost.

mercedes needs spa to be dominant. not close. dominant. otherwise we're all going to spend the next six weeks asking the same question: how did the team with the fastest driver and the most points somehow end up looking like the least in control outfit on the grid?
