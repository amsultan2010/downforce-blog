there's a number that keeps me awake at 3am from riyadh. 242 points. that's how many andrea kimi antonelli has accumulated this season while his nearest competitor sits at 183. in a 24-race calendar, that margin is less "dominant season" and more "everyone else is racing for second."

mercedes isn't just leading the championship. they've effectively ended it midway through the season.

this isn't a hot take wrapped in hyperbole. the gap between first and second in the drivers' championship is 59 points with roughly 13 races remaining (depending on sprint races). even if george russell or lewis hamilton won every single remaining race, neither would catch antonelli. even if red bull suddenly remembered how to build a car, max verstappen would need to see both mercedes drivers retire from every race for the rest of the year. the mathematics are brutal.

the constructor standings tell the real story though. mercedes sits at 425 points. ferrari is second with 338. that's an 87-point cushion. mclaren, supposedly the second-fastest team, is 162 points back. red bull, which won the last three championships, is now a midfield team at 186 points. this isn't competition. this is a top-two manufacturers racing each other while everyone else watches.

what's worse is how they got here. antonelli arrived as a rookie and immediately started winning. he's not scrappy or lucky. he's just faster. russell has played the perfect teammate, consistent and strong but never threatening his teammate's focus. hamilton, despite the narrative around his motivation this year, is still delivering podiums. mercedes built a car that lets them run both strategies simultaneously. they stop worrying about team orders because the car is so good it doesn't matter who finishes ahead.

the fia has a problem on its hands that goes beyond typical dominance. when red bull won three titles in a row with max verstappen, at least there was still fight among the midfield teams. at least other drivers could win races and podiums. this year, mercedes has won so thoroughly that the entire grid is compressed below them. lando norris at fourth place is 83 points behind hamilton. charles leclerc, driving for ferrari, is fifth. oscar piastri is seventh (with a gap of 55 points to the fourth-place driver above him).

here's the uncomfortable part: it's not clear anyone can fix this before 2027. the current regulations have two more seasons to run, and mercedes' advantage appears structural. they're not faster at one track or better in one condition. they're faster everywhere. antonelli's consistency is almost boring to watch (which is its own indictment of how wide the gap has become).

the real question isn't whether antonelli will win the championship (he will). it's whether the sport can survive another season at this pace. tv ratings depend on tension. sponsors want close finishes. drivers want to win. right now, everyone knows where this ends.

monza tomorrow might offer a brief respite (it always does for mid-grid teams), but it won't change the reality. mercedes has built something that shouldn't be possible under current regulations. and the fia should be asking itself whether that's a failure of the rules, the enforcement of the rules, or both.
