kimi antonelli is 19 years old, in his second season of F1, and leading the championship by 43 points after winning four races in a row. i still have to read that sentence twice.

the number that matters this weekend in monaco is the 43, because of how it got there. two weeks ago in montreal the gap was 20, and george russell (the mercedes mercenary himself) was doing everything he could to shrink it. sprint pole, sprint win, pole for the grand prix. after the sprint it was down to 18.

then came sunday. the two of them swapped the lead over and over, locked up at each other, and banged wheels at the final chicane on lap 24. antonelli came out ahead by going off the track, and mercedes told him to hand the place back, which he did. six laps later russell's battery failed while he was leading. james allison, the mercedes technical director, called it a "catastrophic failure." one dead battery, and 18 points became 43.

it was russell's first retirement since the 2024 british grand prix. "it feels like the gods don't want me to be in this fight," he said. i'd have said worse.

so what does mercedes do now? the safe, corporate answer is to back the kid with the 43-point lead and tell george to behave. toto wolff hasn't done that. "more than ever, this fight is on," he said in canada, while adding that the team would "not be a millimeter hesitant of putting the handbrake on" if its points were at risk.

on thursday in monaco both drivers confirmed it: the team isn't setting any rules, as long as they race each other with respect. antonelli's version was that "you can't really put a leash on a driver that is fighting for wins and the championship." russell said they "know the boundaries with one another."

i think it's the right call, and not only because i like chaos. russell hasn't been slow. he won in australia, he beat kimi in sprint qualifying, the sprint and qualifying in canada, and he was leading the race when the battery went. deciding a title by team order in june would be a rubbish way to do it. we all sat through mclaren's papaya rules last year, and nobody enjoyed those.

the catch is that they touched twice in one weekend. "free to race" tends to last right up until both cars are in the wall, and then it becomes a meeting.

which brings us to monaco. qualifying is this afternoon, and around here saturday is basically the race. ferrari topped both practice sessions yesterday (charles leclerc in the first, lewis hamilton in the second), so naturally i expect them to find a way to start third and fourth. and if the two mercedes line up together on the front row, the run down to sainte devote will be the longest few seconds of toto wolff's life.

my prediction: russell takes pole, because he has to, and antonelli is right there next to him. see you after the race.
